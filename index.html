How the pieces fit together

The tools aren't separate apps that happen to share a nav bar. They pass you between them.

1 You hit a word you don't know Reading a story, you meet 歩きました. Paste it into the dictionary and it walks back to 歩く, naming the polite past along the way.
2 The entry shows you the pattern All sixteen forms are right there on the same screen, so you see 歩いて, 歩かない and 歩ける without going anywhere.
3 Then you drill it until it sticks The Conjugator builds forms; Rewind takes them apart. Both remember what you got wrong and bring it back.

Where to Start

New to Japanese? This is the order that works. About an hour a day gets you through it.

  1. 1 Learn the kana Both alphabets, 104 characters. A week or two, and everything after this gets easier.
  2. 2 Get the grammar basics Particles, word order, the polite form — enough to build a sentence on purpose.
  3. 3 Drill the conjugations Every form, on every verb type, until you stop having to think about them.
  4. 4 Hold a conversation Order food, ask directions, get corrected as you go. This is what the rest was for.

Before you ask

Is it really free? Yes. No account, no trial, no paid tier. There's a tip jar if you ever feel like it, and that's the whole business model.
Where does my progress live? In your browser, and nowhere else. Your progress is never uploaded and there's nothing to log into — which also means clearing your browser data clears it, and your phone won't know what your laptop has done. The few things that do leave your browser are listed on the privacy page.
Where does the dictionary come from? It's built from JMdict, the same open dataset Jisho uses, under a Creative Commons licence. The kanji follow the Kodansha Kanji Learner's Course ordering. The conjugation engine, the deconjugator and every explanation on the site are my own.
Does it work on a phone? Every page is built for it. There's no app to install — it's a website, which is the point.
Do I have to study in English? No. The whole interface is in English, German, French and Chinese, properly translated rather than machine-run.

I built Katsuyō Academy because I needed it myself. Scattered textbook chapters, confusing online resources, flashcard apps that didn't quite work the way I wanted — I wanted everything in one place, designed the way I learn best. This is that place. If it helps you too, then it's done its job.
